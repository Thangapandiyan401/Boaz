Briefing temporary staff: a fifteen minute checklist

The gap between a good temp shift and a bad one is usually the first fifteen minutes, not the person.

Managing temp staff 9 July 2026 5 min read

When a temporary shift goes badly, the explanation is almost always “the person was no good”. Sometimes that is true. Far more often the person arrived, was pointed vaguely at a task, was given no idea who to ask when something went wrong, and performed exactly as well as that brief allowed.

A permanent hire absorbs a bad first day because they have three months to recover from it. A temporary worker on a single shift does not. The first fifteen minutes are the entire onboarding, so they are worth treating as a process rather than an afterthought.

Before the shift

Most of the value is created before anyone arrives, in what the shift request actually says. Four details do the heavy lifting:

  • Where to physically go. Not the street address of the building, the door. “Loading dock, rear of the building via Smith Lane” prevents ten minutes of wandering.
  • Who to ask for. A name and a mobile number. Without this, a worker who cannot find anyone will stand in a corridor rather than interrupt someone.
  • What to wear. Including anything they must bring themselves, such as steel caps or blacks.
  • What the work actually is. “Pick and pack” covers a wide range of pace and physicality. Say which end of the range this is.

The fifteen minute induction

When they arrive, run the same four blocks every time. It takes about fifteen minutes and it does not need a manager to deliver it.

1. Site and safety, five minutes

Exits, toilets, break room, first aid, and the two or three hazards specific to this site. Where to put a bag. This is also the point where site-specific induction obligations are met, and those sit with the host business, not the staffing provider.

2. The task, five minutes

Show it rather than describe it. Do one unit of the work in front of them, then watch them do one. Two minutes of demonstration removes an hour of quiet guessing.

3. The standard, two minutes

What “done properly” looks like, and what the common mistake is. Every job has one predictable error. Naming it in advance prevents most of it.

4. Escalation, three minutes

Who to ask, and explicit permission to ask. Say the words: “if you are unsure, stop and come and find me.” Temporary workers routinely guess rather than ask because they do not want to look slow.

The most useful sentence in any induction is an explicit invitation to interrupt you.

Write it down once

Put the four blocks on a single laminated page and keep it where the shift starts. Whoever is on duty can run it without preparation, which means it actually gets run, which is the whole point.

Close the loop at the end

Two minutes at the end: what went well, what to do differently, and whether you would have them back. Rate the shift while it is fresh. That rating is what lets you request the good ones by name next month instead of starting over.
